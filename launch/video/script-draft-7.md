A year ago, AI was a feature every SaaS product had to add, like an assistant or an MCP server. Now it's the thing replacing them.

And that's breaking the SaaS business model, fast.

Traditional SaaS has four parts.

Code. Building software used to be expensive and hard. Now coding agents make it nearly free.

Infrastructure. Running software at scale is expensive, demanding, and fragile. But most users run at a small scale, and that's now nearly free.

Support. Answering users and fixing bugs used to take a support team and engineers. Now agents answer questions and fix bugs automatically.

And product vision. It comes from a founder and a team who obsess over a problem until they understand the solution better than anyone. That part hasn't changed. AI can't recreate vision.

Buy vs. build used to be a question only big companies asked. Now solo creators are asking it too. It's not only the monthly bill. It's paying for a hundred features to only use three.

Building is becoming the default, and building is becoming cloning. Give an agent access to a product, and it copies the whole thing.

But a clone only captures the tip of the iceberg. The vision, taste, constraints, and opinions of the solution are lost, and the user ends up with a worse version.

People want three things: ownership, to use their own AI subscription, and quality solutions.

Introducing SuperGrid, a desktop IDE for creators and builders.

Here's how it works.

First, you connect Muse Code through ACP, so the agent runs on your own subscription. Then you connect your own Cloudflare account, so everything is deployed to and owned by you.

Each workspace you create is its own code repo, and it ships with a database, events, workflows, analytics, and a media library.

Apps are the main part of a workspace, and they're built on top of that core.

You can build your own, or install apps from other builders and founders. The app goes straight into your repo, so you get their complete solution, not a clone.

Apps can be internal tools, like an Ad Designer built on Muse Image, or deployed with your site, like a blog, forms, or landing pages.

Each app can have its own data model, components, pages, routes, and backend code. It can add admin screens for your team, hook into the core systems, and expose its features over MCP, so Muse can use the app natively.

That makes every app a small product, with its own front end and back end, built to fit into what's already there.

The benefit of traditional SaaS is that updates and fixes are easy to ship, and everyone gets them. But with ownership, when everyone has their own copy, that ease disappears.

Muse Code is what makes this possible. It can extend apps, make changes, and merge updates from the builder seamlessly. It checks every update for security issues and fixes anything that breaks. You get every update and keep your changes.

Muse Code becomes the CTO for every user. It's what makes owning the code possible in this new SaaS model.

Platforms like this may exist in every industry. Experts and founders could build solutions that get installed and owned instead of cloned. They'd focus on solving the problem, not on the infrastructure, and still get paid and build a real business around it.

SaaS isn't going away, but it is changing.

The code becomes part of the product that's shipped to and owned by the user.

Products built by the people who understand the problem best, shaped by the people who use them, and powered by the AI they already pay for.
