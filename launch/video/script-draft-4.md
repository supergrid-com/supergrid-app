A year ago, AI was a feature every SaaS raced to add. Now agents aren't being added to SaaS. They're rebuilding it.

I'm Dylan. I dropped out of high school, taught myself to code, and spent my career building platforms that take care of the hard parts so people can focus on the product, from WordPress themes to co-founding ClickFunnels.

Every SaaS has really been four things bundled together: the code, the infrastructure, the support, and the founder's vision. Agents have made the first three nearly free. The vision is the one thing they haven't replaced.

That's pushed the buy-or-build question all the way down to a single creator, and more and more, build means clone. Hand an agent the login and copy the whole product. It's happening to my own products.

But a clone only gets the tip of the iceberg. The vision underneath gets lost, and the builder and the user both lose.

I spent eight months fighting this, then decided to build for it. People want to own their software, use the AI subscription they already pay for, and still get things that are well made.

So I built SuperGrid, an IDE for creators and builders.

You connect Muse over ACP, so it runs on your own subscription, and you connect your Cloudflare account, so you own everything. Each workspace is its own repo, with a database, events, workflows, analytics, and a media library already built in.

Then you add apps. Build them yourself or install them from the App Store, and they go right into your repo. So instead of a clone, you get the builder's whole solution.

Some apps help you run your business from inside the workspace, like an Ad Designer. Others ship with your site, like a blog, forms, or landing pages. And because they live in the same repo, they work together. Drop a form into a blog post and it just works.

Muse can use every app, and build or extend them for you.

There's one catch. SaaS ships a fix once and everyone gets it. When you own the code and change it, every update used to be yours to merge by hand.

Muse Code fixes that. It made your changes, so it knows what changed and why. It merges each update seamlessly, checks it for security issues, and fixes anything that breaks. You stay up to date and keep everything you made your own.

I think Muse Code is the CTO for every creator, and every industry will get tools like this, where expert-built apps get installed instead of cloned and their builders get paid.

SaaS isn't dead. It's changing. Software will belong to the people who use it, and the value stays with the people who care most about the problem.
