A year ago, AI was a feature every SaaS had to add. An assistant, an MCP server. Now agents don't add to SaaS. They rebuild it.

I'm Dylan. I dropped out of high school in 2003 and taught myself to code. WordPress took care of the hard parts, so I could focus on the product. In 2007 I launched my first SaaS, and in 2014 I co-founded ClickFunnels, a platform that did the same for marketers.

Every SaaS is four things: code, infrastructure, support, and the founder's vision. Code is now nearly free. A small team's own scale is nearly free. Agents answer support and fix bugs. The vision is the only part that wasn't replaced.

So buy versus build isn't a big-company question anymore. It reaches a team of one, and price isn't always the reason. More and more, build means clone. Hand an agent the login and copy the whole product. It's happening to my products, to video games, to every industry.

But a clone copies the tip of the iceberg and misses the vision underneath. The builder loses, and so does the user.

I spent eight months fighting this, then decided to build for it. People want to own their software. They want to use the subscription they already pay for, not a token reseller. And they still want quality.

So I built SuperGrid, the IDE for creators and builders.

You connect Muse over ACP to use your own subscription, and your Cloudflare account to own your hosting. Every workspace is its own repo, with three areas: Threads, Apps, and Core. Core is your database, events, workflows, analytics, and library, all running on your own Cloudflare.

Apps tie it together. Build one or install one from the App Store, and it goes straight into your repo, so you get the builder's whole solution, not a clone. And builders only build their solution, because the workspace handles the rest.

Admin apps help you run your business, like an Ad Designer built on Muse Image. Others deploy with your site, like a Blog, Forms, or Landing Pages. Apps share code, so a form drops into any blog post, and every app gives Muse tools to run it.

In Dev mode, Muse builds and extends apps right in your repo, and the admin screens let your team make changes without touching code.

SaaS ships a fix once and everyone gets it. Owned code breaks that. Until now you could get updates or edit the code, not both. Every update and bug fix was yours to merge by hand.

Muse Code knows what changed and why, because it made the changes. So it merges every update seamlessly, checks it for security issues, and fixes anything that breaks. You get every update and keep everything you made your own.

Muse Code is the CTO for every creator. Every industry will get IDEs like this, where expert-built apps get installed instead of cloned, and their builders get paid.

SaaS isn't dead, but it is changing. Software will be owned by the people who use it, and the value stays with the experts who obsess over the problem.
